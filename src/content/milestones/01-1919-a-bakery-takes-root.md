Fred W. Busse's South Bend firm put up a new bread plant at 906 Portage Avenue
and spent **$90,000** doing it — a serious industrial investment, not a corner
bakery. The *South Bend Tribune* called it the most modern plant expert bakery
designers could devise, and one of the finest bread plants in the central west.

The site made sense. Portage Avenue was a streetcar corridor with traffic, transit,
and a dense residential customer base within walking distance — a neighborhood
commercial center with butcher shops, produce sellers, and florists on the same
block.

The building was designed around daylight and cleanliness, part of a national wave
of "sanitary and scientific" bakeries that replaced basement hand-bakeries and
marketed hygiene as a product feature. That design decision — big windows, lots of
light — is the one the next hundred years kept arguing with.
