Busse's independence lasted about three years.

Ward Baking Company — a national operation with roots going back to an Irish
immigrant baker in 1849 Manhattan — moved into South Bend and competed hard,
operating for a time through the **South Bend Bread Company** without announcing
whose money was behind it. Prices were driven down through coupon promotions.
Local bakers said publicly that they did not have the capital to fight back. The
episode is remembered locally as the **South Bend bread war**.

Busse lost. By the mid-1920s the Portage plant was Ward's.

That put this building inside a genuinely notable corporate lineage. Ward's brands
were **Tip-Top** and **Bond**; in 1924 the parent company reorganized as
**Continental Baking**, which picked up **Wonder Bread** the following year and
later launched **Hostess** — eventually the largest commercial bakery in the
United States.
